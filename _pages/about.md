***
Passionate about video games since childhood, I developed a strong interest in computer science early on. After two years of preparatory classes in mathematics and physics, I joined Polytech'Tours, where I specialized in Architecture and Systems. There, I focused on low-level programming (C, C++, CUDA), operating systems, and multithreaded computing.

For my final year of engineering school, I had the opportunity to pursue a double degree in Montréal at the Université de Sherbrooke, enrolling in the DDJV (Diplôme d'études supérieures spécialisées de 2e cycle en développement du jeu vidéo) program.

During this year, I studied computer graphics, AI for video games, and significantly sharpened my C++ skills under the guidance of Professor Patrice Roy. I explored mathematics and physics as applied to real-time 3D environments and took part in two major team projects, both featured in my portfolio. The program was enriched by industry professionals from the Montréal game development scene, and included studio visits to Eidos, Ubisoft, and Panache. We also received specialized workshops in areas like UI/UX, AI, Infography, sound design, and production pipelines, delivered by guest speakers working in the field.

For the past year, I’ve been working as a Gameplay Developer at Wild Blueberry Games in Montréal. I contribute to the development of tools, systems, UI, and gameplay features, while also refactoring and optimizing the existing codebase. I’ve also been involved in porting games to new platforms, which has strengthened my understanding of cross-platform constraints and engine-level systems. This ongoing experience has reinforced my passion for game programming and collaborative development in a professional environment.

I’m now looking to return to France to continue my career as a video game programmer, with a strong interest in both gameplay and engine development. While I’m happy to keep growing as a gameplay programmer, I’m also excited to explore roles that could lead me toward engine-level programming.
